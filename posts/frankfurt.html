Harry Frankfurt on Bullshit

Our reading from Harry Frankfurt is a famous contemporary essays that discusses many themes that emerged in our reading of Socrates' Apology.

Since this is the Philosophy Department's "PL 201 Common Text" for 2026-2027, the faculty got together this summer to discuss this text. You can watch our discussion here: https://jeffreycwitt.com/pl201-common-text/frankfurt-on-bullshit/

As you read, consider the following questions:

What is the distinction that Frankfurt is drawing between "bullshitting" and "lying?" Why is this important? Is there any way in which one is worse than the other, that is, more immoral or more harmful?
What are some of the major areas in our society in which we see a lot of bullshitting going on?
Is there any way to see through it and figure out what is or isn't bullshit?
How does this discussion of bullshit. vs truth-telling relate to our class readings?
How does it relate to the opponents of Socrates in the *Apologia*?
How does Socrates accuse them of "bullshitting" or "lying"? Why?
How does it relate the description of the Sophists seen in our "Introduction to the Sophists" and in the Fragments from Protagoras and Antiphon?
Are these Sophists engaged in truth-seeking, lying, "bullshitting" or something else?
If these thinkers are not interested in truth, what are they interested in? Is this similar or different from the motivation Frankfurt ascribes to those who engage in bullshitting?
Consider the position of Antiphon in particular.
Has Antiphon give up on the idea of truth or does he just disagree with Socrates about what the truth is?
